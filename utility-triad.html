Three vertices.
One infrastructure.

Most discussions of research data infrastructure treat analytic power and privacy compliance as opposing forces. The utility triad reframes this: AU, RU, and GU are not competing goals but three interdependent dimensions of what a mature multimodal data system must address.

SMASH is the reference implementation of this framework — operationalising all three tiers through its canonical event log, MaskBench benchmarks, and embedding substrate.

AU — Analytic Utility Extract maximum signal · enable novel research design
RU — Reflexive Utility Privacy & de-identification · accessible to SSH early career researchers
GU — Generative Utility Synthetic data · model training · deepfakes · responsible by design

Three vertices

AU · Analytic Utility

Analytic Utility

The signal extractable from a corpus using available methods. Every pipeline decision — masking, segmentation, extraction — either preserves or reduces it. Maximising AU enables novel research designs: cross-corpus comparisons, longitudinal kinematic tracking, gesture–prosody synchrony analysis at scale.

RU · Reflexive Utility

Reflexive Utility

Privacy and de-identification made accessible to SSH early career researchers. RU is the capacity to reason explicitly about what each masking choice costs in analytic quality — so researchers balance rather than toggle. SMASH operationalises RU through MaskBench benchmarks and provenance metadata that travel with every event in the log.

GU · Generative Utility

Generative Utility

The capacity to synthesize new data and representations using generative AI. Use cases span synthetic data generation, model training on privacy-preserved corpora, and — at the risk frontier — deepfake production that responsible governance must anticipate. The most powerful tier and the most governance-demanding: GU requires explicit safeguards against hallucination, re-identification, and epistemic laundering.

Theoretical grounding

Extending Maass et al.
into multimodal
infrastructure

The utility triad extends the data-driven / theory-driven framework of Maass, Parsons, Purao, Storey & Woo (2018, JAIS) into the specific context of multimodal data infrastructure. Their four IS challenges — reconciling knowledge creation modes, selecting data for theory testing, solving cross-disciplinary problems, sharing data across teams — map cleanly onto the three vertices: AU addresses challenges 1 and 2; RU addresses challenge 4 (sharing under privacy constraints); GU opens challenge 5 (synthesis), which was beyond scope in 2018.

The base edge of the triangle — RU ↔ GU — is the governance frontier: as generative AI increases the power to produce synthetic corpora, the reflexive capacity to audit and constrain that production becomes existentially important. Infrastructure that operationalises only AU without RU is incomplete; infrastructure that enables GU without RU is dangerous.

SMASH is the reference implementation. The canonical event log ensures that every automated extraction, masking operation, and synthetic generation is traceable, auditable, and reversible — making the triad not a diagram but a design specification.

Maass, W., Parsons, J., Purao, S., Storey, V. C., & Woo, C. (2018). Data-Driven Meets Theory-Driven Research in the Era of Big Data. Journal of the Association for Information Systems, 19(12), 1253–1273. doi:10.17705/1jais.00526
