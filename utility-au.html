AU — Analytic Utility

The signal extractable from a corpus using available analytic methods. Every pipeline decision either preserves or reduces it. Maximising AU enables novel research designs that are otherwise computationally or logistically impossible.

Primary goal · apex of the triad

What analytic utility means in practice

Not just "quality data" — legible tradeoffs

Analytic utility is not a binary property of a dataset. It is a function of the relationship between a corpus, a set of analytic methods, and a research question. The same video corpus has high AU for gesture stroke detection and lower AU for facial action unit coding after face-blurring — because the analytic pipeline and the masking operation interact.

SMASH makes this interaction explicit. Rather than treating masking as a prior step that happens before analysis, the canonical event log records every masking decision as a typed, provenanced event — which means AU can be assessed relative to specific pipeline configurations.

This is what distinguishes AU from raw data quality: AU is always relative to a research purpose, and responsible infrastructure must preserve that relativity rather than collapsing it.

SMASH modules that maximise AU

L0 · Ingest
Video, audio, transcript, and annotation import. All formats normalised to a single event schema. Provenance set to auto or human at import time.
L2 · Masking
SAM2, YOLO-based segmentation, MaskBench evaluation. AU cost of each masking strategy is computed and stored alongside the mask operation in the event log.
L3 · Extractors
Whisper (ASR), pyannote (diarization), MediaPipe / OpenPose / ViTPose (pose), openSMILE / Praat (audio features), BERTopic (topic modelling), JEGAL (cross-modal embedding).
L4 · Cross-modal Analytics
Gesture–prosody coupling, acoustic–gestural synchrony, trouble moments detection. Output is new events in the canonical log, composable with human annotations.
L5 · Run Management
YAML pipeline manifests, smash reproduce, inference cache. Every analysis run is reproducible from the manifest + event log alone.
L6 · UI Shell
Carbon Design System interface. Lite Wizard for non-technical researchers; full Pipeline editor for advanced users. Baselines and Results Viewer for AU comparison across runs.

Research designs AU enables

Cross-corpus comparison

The canonical event schema is corpus-agnostic. Once two corpora are ingested and extracted, their event streams can be aligned by timestamp and modality — enabling comparisons across recording conditions, languages, or speaker populations that were previously blocked by format incompatibility.

Longitudinal kinematic tracking

Pose estimation events carry speaker identity, session metadata, and corpus provenance. Tracking kinematic change over time — within a clinical population, across an educational intervention, or through a conversation — requires reliable event linkage across recording sessions that SMASH makes native.

Maass et al. 2018 — Challenges 1 & 2

Maass et al.'s first challenge — reconciling data-driven and theory-driven knowledge creation — is directly addressed by AU as SMASH operationalises it. The canonical event log carries both: automated feature extraction (data-driven) and human annotation with codebook references (theory-driven) co-exist as events with typed provenance. A researcher can impose McNeill gesture types onto MediaPipe keypoint events without discarding the confidence scores the estimator produced.

The second challenge — selecting data and analytical techniques appropriate for theory testing — maps onto AU's relativity to research purpose. The Baselines module in SMASH lets researchers compare the analytic output of different extraction pipelines on the same corpus before committing to one, making technique selection an empirical rather than incidental decision.
