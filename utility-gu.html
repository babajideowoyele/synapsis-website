GU — Generative Utility

The capacity to synthesize new data, representations, and research artifacts from existing corpora using generative AI. The most powerful tier of the triad — and the most governance-demanding. Requires explicit safeguards against hallucination, re-identification, and epistemic laundering.

Frontier tier · bottom-right vertex · open research agenda

Opportunities and risks — two faces of generative utility

Synthetic corpus generation

Generate pose-conditioned or speech-conditioned synthetic video clips to augment training sets, fill corpus gaps, or simulate rare interaction patterns. Privacy-safe by construction — no real participants in the generated data.

data augmentationsynthetic training

Deepfake production

The same synthesis capability that generates synthetic training data can reconstruct identifiable faces from masked corpora, or produce convincing false recordings of real participants. A governance failure at this frontier undermines every RU protection in the infrastructure.

re-identification riskgovernance critical

Model training on de-identified corpora

Train gesture detection, emotion recognition, or speaker diarization models on masked corpora — using SMASH's provenance schema to ensure the masking history travels with the training data, making the model's data lineage auditable.

model trainingdata lineage

Epistemic laundering

LLM-assisted annotation — asking a language model to fill in missing transcription, label gesture types, or summarize interactions — can introduce systematic hallucination that is then treated as ground truth if the provenance chain does not record the synthesis step.

hallucination riskprovenance critical

Cross-modal representation learning

JEGAL (Joint Embedding for Gesture and Language Alignment) learns a shared representational space across gesture kinematics and speech tokens. This opens generative use cases: gesture-to-speech synthesis, speech-conditioned gesture prediction, cross-corpus embedding alignment.

JEGALcross-modalembedding

Synthetic identity construction

Combining voice synthesis, pose generation, and face swap in a single pipeline can construct a synthetic identity that appears to be a real research participant — a failure mode with serious implications for clinical, legal, and educational research contexts.

identity synthesisfuture governance

⚠ Governance principle: RU must precede GU

Every generative operation in SMASH writes a provenance: synthetic event to the canonical log. This ensures the audit chain that RU built is not erased by GU. Infrastructure that enables GU without an operationalised RU layer is not merely incomplete — it is a governance liability for the institutions that deploy it.

JEGAL — The GU substrate in SMASH

Joint Embedding for
Gesture and Language Alignment

JEGAL is a cross-modal attention model that computes token-level coupling scores between gesture stroke events and co-occurring word tokens from the transcription stream. Its output is a G×W coupling heatmap — the intensity at (g, w) representing the degree to which gesture event g and word token w co-occur systematically across the corpus.

The representational space JEGAL learns is the substrate for GU: once gesture and speech are embedded in a shared space, generative operations become tractable — speech-conditioned gesture prediction, gesture-conditioned transcription scoring, and eventually full cross-modal synthesis.

All JEGAL outputs are stored as typed events in the canonical log with provenance: auto and a model version tag, making them subject to the same audit chain as any other extraction.

Open research agenda for GU

Governance frameworks for synthetic corpora

No agreed standard exists for how synthetic research data should be labelled, audited, or cited. The SMASH provenance schema is a starting point — the research agenda is to make it interoperable with emerging standards from EOSC, CLARIN, and ISO/IEC AI governance frameworks.

EOSCCLARINISO AI

Deepfake detection benchmarks for SSH

Deepfake detection benchmarks exist for news media and social platforms but not for the specific modalities of SSH research video (close-up conversational data, clinical recordings, classroom interaction). MaskBench v2 will include synthetic detection as a benchmark track.

MaskBench v2detection

Differential privacy for generative fine-tuning

Fine-tuning generative models on private SSH corpora risks memorising participant data. Differential privacy techniques can bound this risk — but their application to multimodal (video + audio + text) corpora is an open engineering and governance problem.

Beyond Maass et al. 2018 — Challenge 5

Maass et al.'s four IS challenges were framed before large-scale generative AI became a research tool. GU opens a fifth challenge they did not anticipate: how to govern synthesis — the production of new research artifacts from existing data using models that may hallucinate, memorise, or re-identify. This challenge sits at the intersection of information systems, AI ethics, and SSH methodology, and it will define the field's infrastructure priorities for the next decade.

The utility triad — and SMASH as its reference implementation — positions AU and RU as necessary prerequisites for responsible GU. Infrastructure that reaches for GU without having operationalised AU and RU is building on an unstable foundation: high synthesis capacity with low audit coverage is precisely the failure mode that erodes public trust in research data systems.
