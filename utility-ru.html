RU — Reflexive Utility

Privacy and de-identification made accessible to SSH early career researchers. The capacity to reason explicitly about what each masking choice costs in analytic quality — so researchers balance the two rather than treating privacy compliance as a binary gate.

Enabling condition · bottom-left vertex

MaskBench — Operationalising reflexive utility

Not compliance.
Principled balancing.

Most discussions of privacy and research data treat de-identification as a prerequisite: first protect, then analyse. Reflexive utility reframes this: what does this masking strategy cost this specific analysis pipeline? That is an empirical question, and MaskBench provides the benchmark to answer it.

A researcher with high RU can make statements like: "face-blur preserves 91% of MediaPipe pose quality but only 78% of ViTPose quality on this corpus." She can then choose the masking strategy appropriate to her estimator — not just to her institution's IRB template.

This is especially important for SSH early career researchers who lack the computational background to independently assess masking costs — and who therefore tend to over-protect (blocking data sharing entirely) or under-protect (applying minimal masking without understanding the re-identification risk).

RU workflow for SSH early career researchers

1

Assess your data

Identify what modalities you have (face, body, audio, transcript) and what your IRB or GDPR obligation requires. SMASH's Ingest module creates a provenance record at import.

2

Run MaskBench on a sample

Select a representative 10-minute sample. Run MaskBench to get analytic utility scores for each masking strategy × each extractor you plan to use. Takes ~15 minutes on CPU.

3

Choose your masking strategy

Select the strategy that preserves sufficient AU for your research question while meeting your institution's minimum requirement. SMASH records the choice as a typed event.

4

Apply masking with provenance

SMASH runs the masking pipeline and logs every operation: which frame, which algorithm, which confidence threshold. The log is your IRB artifact.

5

Archive and deposit

The masked corpus + event log constitute a FAIR-compliant deposit package. DANS / SURF / OSF accept the package. Future users know exactly what was applied.

What RU makes possible

Corpus sharing across institutions

The event log's provenance chain gives receiving institutions the evidence they need to evaluate the dataset for their own IRB. A corpus with high RU documentation can be shared, re-used, and cited — one without it cannot.

Masking-aware analysis pipelines

Because masking events appear in the same event log as extraction events, downstream analysis can be conditioned on masking type. A comparison of gesture detection across masked vs. unmasked recordings becomes a standard query, not a custom script.

The audit log produced by SMASH — which frames, which algorithm, which parameters — is the material evidence that IRBs, data stewards, and journal data availability reviewers need. RU makes the evidence automatic.

Maass et al. 2018 — Challenge 4 · RU ↔ GU governance frontier

Maass et al.'s fourth challenge — sharing data and models across research teams — is where privacy intersects with infrastructure most directly in SSH contexts. Their framework, developed primarily for text and tabular data, treats sharing as a matter of format and access control. For video data, sharing requires prior transformation, and transformation has AU costs. RU addresses this gap: it makes the transformation auditable and the cost visible.

The RU/GU base of the utility triad is the governance frontier because as generative utility increases — as it becomes possible to synthesize new data from masked corpora — the reflexive layer becomes existentially important. Without RU, GU can generate synthetic data that traces back to ethically problematic source material without any record that it did so.
